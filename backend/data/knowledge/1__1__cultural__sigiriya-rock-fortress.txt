Sigiriya — The Lion Rock Fortress
=================================

Sigiriya, located in the Matale District of Sri Lanka's Central Province, is one of the most striking archaeological sites in South Asia. The site is dominated by a 200-metre column of weathered granite that rises abruptly from the surrounding plain, and which served as the seat of King Kashyapa I from approximately 477 to 495 CE.

History and Significance
------------------------

King Kashyapa, the son of King Dhatusena, built the fortress at the summit of the rock to escape both the wrath of his half-brother Moggallana and the political consequences of having killed his father. The royal complex Kashyapa constructed combined a fortified palace with elaborate gardens, frescoes, and a giant lion-shaped gateway from which the site takes its modern name (Sigiriya = Sinhagiri = "Lion Rock"). After Kashyapa's death the site reverted to a Buddhist monastery and remained in religious use until the 14th century, when it was abandoned and largely forgotten until British surveyors rediscovered it in the 1830s.

Visitor Experience
------------------

Most tourists begin the climb early in the morning to avoid both the heat and the midday crowds. The route ascends through the symmetrical water gardens, past the famous mirror wall (which still bears graffiti from 8th-century visitors), and along a metal staircase to the summit plateau. The frescoes of the celestial maidens, painted on a sheltered rock face about halfway up, are among Sri Lanka's most photographed art works. The full ascent typically takes 90 minutes and the descent another 45.

Best Time to Visit
------------------

The dry season runs from May through September and offers the most reliable weather for the climb. The monsoon months of October and November can be both slippery and humid, although early-morning visits are still feasible. During the peak season (December–March) the site receives over 4,000 visitors a day; arriving at opening time (07:00) is strongly advised.

Practical Tips
--------------

* Bring at least 1.5 litres of water per person — there are no shops on the rock itself.
* Wear closed-toe shoes with good grip; the upper stairs are steep and exposed.
* The combined Sigiriya + Pidurangala ticket offers excellent value for the sunset view.
* Photography is permitted everywhere except inside the fresco gallery, where flash is forbidden to protect the pigment.
