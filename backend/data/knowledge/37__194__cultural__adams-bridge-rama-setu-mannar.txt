# Adam's Bridge (Rama Setu)

**District:** Mannar (Northern Province)
**Category:** cultural / heritage site
**Best months to visit:** May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep
**District peak season:** May, Jun, Jul, Aug
**Climate zone:** dry
**Entry fee:** Free

## Overview
Chain of limestone shoals stretching towards India — myth says Rama crossed it; today an ecotourism boat trip.

## Practical tips
- Bring water and a wide-brimmed hat; ancient cities offer little shade. Most sites accept photography for personal use without a fee.

## How to get there
Most travellers reach Adam's Bridge (Rama Setu) via Mannar town. From Colombo it is roughly 320 km by road; public buses run daily from Pettah, and intercity trains serve the major hill-country and coastal districts. Tuk-tuks cover the last mile from Mannar town.

## Background (excerpted from Wikipedia)
Adam's Bridge, also known as Rama's Bridge or Rama Setu, is a chain of natural limestone shoals between Pamban Island, also known as Rameswaram Island, off the southeastern coast of Tamil Nadu, India, and Mannar Island, off the northwestern coast of Sri Lanka. Geological evidence suggests that the bridge was formerly a land connection between India and Sri Lanka.
The feature is 48 km (30 mi) long and separates the Gulf of Mannar (southwest) from the Palk Strait (northeast). Some regions of the bridge are dry, and the sea in the area rarely exceeds 1 metre (3 ft) in depth, making it quite difficult for boats to pass over it.
